02 Executive Summary & The National Mandate

The healthcare system of Bangladesh serves 170+ million citizens across a three-tiered delivery pyramid: primary Community Clinics and Upazila Health Complexes (UHCs), secondary District Sadar Hospitals, and apex tertiary medical college hospitals. While Bangladesh has achieved historic milestones in reducing maternal and child mortality, the healthcare apparatus remains crippled by fragmentation, catastrophic out-of-pocket costs, and severe systemic leakages.

Under the Digital Health Strategy 2023–2028, the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) has initiated the National Health ID (HID) and the distribution of physical e-Health Cards across six designated pilot districts. However, a profound implementation vacuum threatens this initiative: the physical cards arrive at rural clinics, but front-line healthcare workers possess no software to read, record, or act upon them. The cards remain inert plastic.

💡 The Core Thesis of CareLoop (BD-HealthLoop)
CareLoop delivers the definitive, interoperable clinical operating system designed to activate the National Health Card from Day One. It connects rural ticket counters, busy government physicians, diagnostic laboratories, and pharmacies into one unbroken, automated care loop. By building natively on BD Core HL7 FHIR R4 and engineering for extreme rural constraints (8-hour load-shedding and 26% smartphone access), CareLoop bridges policy and the frontline clinic.

Core Strategic Deliverables

  • Sub-Minute OPD Triage: Accelerate rural outpatient check-in from 15 minutes of manual logging to a 5-second QR scan and automated token generation.
  • Antimicrobial Stewardship: Embed real-time WHO AWaRe classification guidance inside the prescription workflow to arrest dangerous antibiotic resistance.
  • Universal Access for the Disconnected: Provide complete digital continuity for patients without smartphones through signed physical QR cards and automated 2G Bangla SMS / USSD fallback.
  • Syndicate & Middlemen Elimination: Dismantle hospital broker ("dalal") networks through cryptographically locked tokens, cellular geofencing, and public waiting-room queue displays.

3. Care Level Referral Patients bypass Upazila complexes and travel directly to tertiary medical colleges in Dhaka, causing extreme overcrowding. 59% of tertiary inpatients self-refer; 58% of rural patients are unaware a tiered referral system exists (DGHS Survey).

06 Breakthrough Technical Innovations for Bangladesh Realities

CareLoop was engineered specifically to triumph over the harsh field conditions of rural Bangladesh that routinely cause traditional foreign health software to fail:

⚡ 1. Resilient Offline-First PWA Architecture (CRDT State Synchronization)

Rural Upazila Health Complexes routinely experience 6 to 8 hours of daily electrical load-shedding and sudden fiber outages. CareLoop's web frontends operate as Progressive Web Apps (PWAs) utilizing IndexedDB local storage. OPD ticket counters are assigned partitioned token blocks (e.g., Counter 1 owns 1–500; Counter 2 owns 501–1000). When internet connectivity severs, registration, queue ticketing, and clinical prescriptions continue uninterrupted. Upon reconnection, Conflict-free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs) and UUIDv7 sequential timestamps merge records with zero data collisions.

🪪 2. Ed25519 Cryptographically Signed Physical QR Card Identity

To prevent dependence on live internet for identity verification, the physical paper or PVC e-Health Card issued to the citizen contains an Ed25519 cryptographically signed compact QR code. The QR payload encodes the patient's 9-digit Health ID, emergency blood group, severe allergies, and chronic conditions signed by the DGHS Master Key. Any Community Clinic worker or paramedic can scan the card with an offline mobile camera, immediately verifying authenticity without sending a single network packet.

🦠 3. Real-Time WHO AWaRe Antibiotic Stewardship Decision Support

Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) threatens to render common infections untreatable in South Asia. CareLoop incorporates a zero-latency clinical decision rule engine based on the WHO AWaRe classification (Access, Watch, Reserve). If a physician enters a Watch-category antibiotic (e.g., Cefixime or Ceftriaxone) for acute viral bronchitis or uncomplicated watery diarrhea, CareLoop instantly displays a visual guidance banner recommending first-line Access-category alternatives (e.g., Amoxicillin). The system does not lock the doctor's authority, but requires a 1-click clinical override reason, establishing transparent clinical governance.

🛡️ 4. Anti-Broker ("Anti-Dalal") Queue & Geofence Protection

In major government hospitals, corrupt brokers hoard morning OPD tickets and sell them to illiterate rural patients for ৳200–৳500. CareLoop eliminates this exploit through three coordinated barriers: (1) cryptographic binding to verified NID/HID; (2) 50-meter GPS/cellular geofencing on mobile check-in; and (3) real-time waiting-room LCD screens displaying current queue tokens.

📱 5. Universal 2G Bangla SMS & USSD (*16263#) Inclusion Bridge

Recognizing that 74% of rural women do not own smartphones, CareLoop guarantees that no patient is excluded. When laboratory results, emergency referrals, or prescription summaries are generated, the system automatically dispatches a secure, plain-text Bangla SMS (e.g., "আপনার রক্তের সিবিসি রিপোর্ট তৈরি হয়েছে। রিপোর্ট দেখতে পিন নম্বর ৮৪৯২ ব্যবহার করুন।"). Basic phone users can check queue serials or confirm appointments via USSD (*16263#).

08 Security, Privacy & National Data Governance

Health data represents the most sensitive category of citizen information. CareLoop is architected in rigorous alignment with the Bangladesh Cyber Security Act 2023 and the DGHS Health Data Governance Framework:

Security Layer Implementation Protocol Regulatory Compliance
Data at Rest Encryption All database volumes, PostgreSQL tables, and S3 document vaults encrypted with AES-256. National Cyber Security Policy 2023
Data in Transit Encryption Mandatory TLS 1.3 transport encryption across all mobile, desktop, and API endpoints; HSTS enforced. BTRC & DGHS API Security Standards
Patient Consent Architecture Doctors cannot view longitudinal patient history without explicit citizen authorization via OTP or physical card QR scan. DGHS Shared Health Record (SeHR) Consent Mandate
Role-Based Access Control Strict principle of least privilege; clerks see queue tokens only; pharmacists see active prescriptions; doctors see medical history. ISO/IEC 27001 & ISO 27799 (Health Informatics)
Immutable Audit Trail Every record access, modification, or export is cryptographically signed and logged as a FHIR AuditEvent resource. Section 34, Bangladesh Cyber Security Act

Disaster Recovery & High-Availability Architecture

To support 24/7 national healthcare operations without catastrophic outages, CareLoop implements carrier-grade infrastructure resilience:

  • Data Sovereignty: Primary databases hosted exclusively within the sovereign territory of Bangladesh at the Tier-III National Data Center (BCC Kaliakoir).
  • Recovery Targets: Recovery Point Objective (RPO) < 5 minutes; Recovery Time Objective (RTO) < 15 minutes.
  • Family Privacy Mode: In shared-phone households, sensitive records (reproductive health, psychiatry) require an independent secondary PIN.
🔒 Patient Rights & The SeHR Consent Gateway
Citizens maintain unconditional ownership of their health information. Through the Citizen Health Portal (Module 03) or SMS authorization, patients can grant, restrict, or revoke access to any historical medical record at any time.

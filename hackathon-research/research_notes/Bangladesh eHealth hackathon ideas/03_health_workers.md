# Bangladesh Health-Worker Pain Points and Proven Foreign Digital Tools (Phase-2 Group B)

Research date: 26 Oct 2026. Sources are public pages. Many figures were retrieved through search-engine summaries and were not re-read in full. Those are marked "(via search abstract)". Treat them as needing a quick re-check before you put them on a pitch slide. [uncertain] = not independently verified.

## Q1. Bangladesh pain points of practising health workers

### Takeaway
Front-line workers in Bangladesh face five problems: very short consultations under heavy load, chronic vacancies, routine violence, no relicensing/CME, and almost no point-of-care decision support. Most first-contact care (village doctors, drug-shop sellers, CHCPs) is delivered by people with little or no formal clinical training. Their digital tools are mostly for reporting, not for clinical support.

### Cited Findings

**Workload and patient overload**
- Average primary-care consultation length in Bangladesh is 48 seconds, the shortest of 67 countries. The range ran up to 22.5 min in Sweden (Irving et al., BMJ Open, Nov 2017; systematic review, ~28.5M consultations) — [BMJ Open blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bmjopen/2017/11/08/primary-care-consultations-last-less-than-5-minutes-for-half-the-worlds-population/); [PMC5695512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5695512)
- Rural physicians self-reported a mean of ~30 patients/day (SD ~18) in public-sector settings (BMC Health Serv Res, 2017) (via search abstract) — [BMC HSR 2017](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-017-2722-1/tables/1)
- The Keraniganj UHC medical officer reported 450–500 OPD patients daily even with all 15 posts filled. Barguna's two upazilas (~0.5M people) had 4 doctors, and Shyamnagar (>400,000 people) had 4 MOs. Nationally there were 4,770 doctors against 10,640 upazila MO posts, i.e. 55.2% vacant per the DGHS website (Prothom Alo, 6 Jun 2018) — [Prothom Alo 2018](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/Unhealthy-state-of-health-services-for-rural)
- Bauphal (Patuakhali) 50-bed UHC: 4 doctors in post against 15 sanctioned, serving ~4.5 lakh people. A single doctor was seen handling a crowded OPD (Observer, Oct 2025) (via search abstract) — [Observer BD](https://observerbd.com/news/547528)
- Interns at Bogura's 500-bed hospital said they were serving ~3,000 patients (TBS, 16 Jun 2026) — [TBS 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1465051)

**Burnout**
- Chattogram frontline doctors (n=168, Jun–Aug 2020): overall burnout 55.4% (95% CI 47.5–63.0). Higher burnout was linked to age 25–29, female sex and medical-officer rank (PLoS ONE 2022) — [PMC9681110](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9681110/)
- ICU physicians and nurses (93 MDs + 247 nurses, 12 ICUs): 21.3% had moderate–high burnout (BCCJ; year not confirmed) — [Banglajol BCCJ](https://www.banglajol.info/index.php/BCCJ/article/view/47702)
- Doctors and nurses, an online survey in 2021, measured burnout, anxiety and depression (BMJ Open, Mar 2024). The prevalence figures were not retrieved — [BMJ Open e079350](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/14/3/e079350)

**Documentation / reporting burden**
- In a qualitative DHIS2 study in two districts, staff named these barriers: staff shortages, slow internet, frequent DHIS2 version changes, and keeping manual and electronic systems side by side. Parallel HMIS reporting of the same RMNCAH indicators "increases the reporting workload" (BMC HSR, May 2020) — [PMC7249629](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7249629/)

**Clinical decision support / guideline access**
- No official DGHS clinician app for standard treatment guidelines was found. DGHS publishes guidelines as PDFs, e.g. the Malaria regimen 2017 and Dengue 2018 — [DGHS guideline page](https://old.dghs.gov.bd/index.php/en/publications/guideline)
- The DGHS NCD Programme's 2015 desk guide (CVD, HTN, T2DM, asthma/COPD) for UHC physicians exists only as a PDF — [WHO NCD desk guide 2015](https://extranet.who.int/ncdccs/Data/BGD_D1_BGD_NCD%20Desk%20Guide_2015.pdf)
- NCDC-DGHS and icddr,b developed a national primary-care CKD protocol. icddr,b is building a digital version on the government platform (TBS; status unknown) — [TBS CKD protocol](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1385381)
- The BADAS diabetes guideline has an app, "Diabetes Journey". It is a BADAS/Novo Nordisk product, not a government one — [TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/23169); [CGHR-BADAS](https://cghr-badas.org/badas-diabetes-care-guideline-and-diabetes-journey/)
- National formulary: a Dhaka Tribune report says the DGDA formulary was not updated for 8 years after 2006, leaving doctors dependent on manufacturer literature and sales reps (article date not shown; likely ~2014) [uncertain on date] — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/feature/travel/61695/no-national-formulary-in-eight-years)

**CME / relicensing (verified: no CME requirement found)**
- BMDC's renewal requirements are paperwork only: form, fee, original certificate, MBBS/BDS certificate, TIN, tax return and NID. No CME credits are listed — [BMDC renewal](https://www.bmdc.org.bd/reg-renewal)
- BMDC (Nov 2024): 134,568 registered physicians, 14,323 dentists and 2,918 medical assistants. About 36,000 physicians practise without renewed registration. BMDC does not know how many registered with fake documents. Only 147 complaints were received since 2010, and 17 physicians were punished — [Prothom Alo, Nov 2024](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/jkx47oy065)
- A regional review says mandatory CPD/revalidation "is yet to be a feature" for South-East Asian doctors, and that Bangladesh was only "in the process of / about to initiate" a credit-hour system (c. 2019) — [Karunathilake, SEA revalidation](https://www.res.cmb.ac.lk/medicine/indika-karunathilake/wp-content/uploads/sites/91/2019/12/Are-South-East-Asian-Countries-Ready-for-Revalidation.pdf)

**Referral and second-opinion gaps**
- At tertiary hospitals, of institutionally referred patients ~25% came from UHCs, 22% from district hospitals, 42% from private clinics and ~10% from community clinics. Reasons for referral: inadequate facilities (53%), inadequate treatment (47%), difficult cases (44%), and no expert physician (31%). Self-referral was 68% at private and 56% at government hospitals (BMC HSR, May 2024) (via search abstract) — [BMC HSR 2024](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-024-11115-8.pdf)
- Savar UHC dengue referrals: barriers were ambulance shortage, no feedback system and skill shortage. 25% of 36 referred patients were dissatisfied with UHC–hospital communication (BRAC University) — [BRACU dspace](https://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/items/3cfefc17-e696-48d8-80c1-b06aa715a7c0)
- A Rangpur referral pilot (2014) failed, as did attempts in 2016 and 2019 (opinion piece) — [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/views/opinions/why-we-need-a-referral-system-in-healthcare)

**Violence against doctors**
- CIPRB/ICRC 2023 survey, 1,703 health personnel, 61 facilities, all 8 divisions: >80% had experienced or witnessed violence. 74.8% of it was verbal. 71.9% was by attendants/relatives. 89% said a reporting system existed, but only ~25% reported incidents (cited in TBS, 16 Jun 2026) — [TBS 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1465051)
- DMCH, 441 physicians: 67.3% had experienced workplace violence (84.5% psychological, 13.5% physical). Relatives were the perpetrators in 66% (BMJ Open 2024, cited in TBS) — [TBS 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1465051)
- Dated incidents:
  - Jamalpur, Mar 2024 (interns)
  - Chattogram NICU, Apr 2024
  - Cox's Bazar CCU, Sep 2024 (hospital-wide shutdown)
  - Pabna, Jul 2025 (female intern)
  - Mongla, Mar 2026
  - DMCH, Apr 2026
  - Shariatpur Sadar, May 2026
  - Rangpur MCH, ~13 Jun 2026
  - Barishal, 10 Jun 2026

  All from [TBS 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1465051)
- BMA Chattogram called a work abstention in Apr 2024 over attacks, including one at Patia General Hospital — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/nation/344321/attacks-on-doctors-bma-ctg-unit-announces-work)
- DMCH work abstention after 3 doctors were attacked by peers of a deceased student (Aug 2024) — [Observer BD](https://observerbd.com/news/468970)

**Absenteeism (verified, but the data are old)**
- Chaudhury & Hammer, "Ghost Doctors" (World Bank WP 2003 / WBER 2004): average absenteeism was 35% across job categories. Physicians were absent 40% of the time at larger clinics and 74% at single-doctor subcentres. Rural vacancy rate was 26% — [World Bank OKR](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/bb381354-fd28-5ee4-940f-6171ac390013/full); [DFID PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08cc2ed915d3cfd0015c4/ChaudhuryandHammer_Ghost_doctors_1_.pdf)
- Correction to the brief: the "~40%" figure is the physician rate at larger facilities, not the overall rate. The overall figure is 35%. No 2019+ national absenteeism survey was found.

**Nurse:doctor ratio and shortage**
- Doctors + nurses + midwives = 12.78 per 10,000, against the WHO's 44.5. The doctor:nurse:midwife ratio is 1:0.75:0.74, against the WHO's 1:3:5. ~75% of doctors and nurses are urban, while 62% of the population is rural. 46% of nurses want to work abroad. ~72,000 doctor/nurse/midwife posts are vacant (Daily Star editorial citing a government-funded study, 21 Jun 2026) — [Daily Star 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/opinion/editorial/news/shortage-nurses-urgent-crisis-4203666)
- 2023: ~7.9 doctors and 5.7 nursing personnel per 10,000 (Financial Express) (via search abstract) — [FE](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/bangladesh-trails-south-asia-in-health-workforce-density)
- >34% of sanctioned health posts are vacant (Bonik Barta; date not shown) — [Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/jlhin00R6bKyinX4)

**Community clinics / CHCPs**
- 32 sampled community clinics saw 2,238 patients/day in total, ~70 per clinic (my arithmetic) (Riaz et al., APJHM, Jun 2021) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ecd5cdd2b63a49848b10cfe31a41f8ee)
- ~490,000 people use community clinics daily. Most clinics have no MBBS doctor, and CHCPs provide the services (Dhaka Tribune) — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/health/413839/inside-bangladesh%E2%80%99s-community-clinics-promise)
- One Gournadi CHCP usually sees 10–12 patients/day, with spikes of 53–80. Hours are 8am–1pm (Daily Star, ~2020; anecdotal) — [Daily Star](https://online92.thedailystar.net/frontpage/news/exposed-virus-risk-1896565)
- 14,213 of 15,564 community clinics were using a DHIS2-tracker e-HIS to register women for cervical screening (Apr 2024) — [BMC Public Health 2025](https://researchexchange.ocrahope.org/papers/s12889-025-22668-6)
- mPower is building a CBHC digital system for CHCPs with unique IDs and clinical history, integrated with HMIS/SHR/DHIS2 (2018–present) — [mPower](https://mpower-social.com/digitization-of-services-at-community-based-health-care-cbhc-under-the-government-of-bangladesh-and-integration-with-dghss-national-health-management-information-systems-hmis-human-resou/)
- A CHCP e-learning platform was built by Riseup Labs with CBHC/WHO. The contract was signed Sep 2022 and UAT ran in Sylhet in Feb 2023 — [Riseup Labs](https://riseuplabs.com/chcp-uat/)
- eRegMat cluster trial (Matlab): an eRegistry with clinical decision support (CDS), dashboards and client communication at community clinics/FWCs — [ISRCTN69491836](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/ISRCTN69491836)

**Informal providers / village doctors**
- In Chakaria, 65% of patients consulted village doctors, who were first-line care for 67%. Only 14% consulted MBBS doctors (PMC 2014) — [PMC4259872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4259872)
- Village doctors are >80% of providers in rural areas (2014). Over 65% of the population gets first-line care from village doctors (J Glob Health 2018) — [JOGH 2018](https://jogha.org/documents/issue201802/jogh-08-020413.htm)
- Rangpur tertiary inpatients (n=197): the first contact was an informal provider for 46.2% and a registered doctor for 42.1% (JBCPS 2026) — [Banglajol JBCPS](https://banglajol.info/index.php/JBCPS/article/view/91557)
- A national telephone survey of village doctors exists (BMC HSR 2023). Headline figures were not retrieved — [BMC HSR 2023](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-023-09972-w/tables/1)

**Drug shops / pharmacists**
- DGDA–MSH accreditation (2015) has two tiers. A Model Pharmacy (L1) needs a Grade A graduate pharmacist. A Model Medicine Shop (L2) needs at least a Grade C pharmacist — [Frontiers Pharmacol 2023](https://frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2023.1139632/full); [MSH](https://msh.org/story/specialized-approach-to-training-medicine-dispensers-shows-positive-results-in-bangladesh/)
- Licensed outlet counts conflict:
  - 106,919 licensed plus a similar number unlicensed (DGDA 2017, cited in Frontiers 2023)
  - >150,000 licensed plus an equal number unlicensed (MSH)
  - 173,525 licensed, of which 234 Model Pharmacies and 51,439 Model Medicine Shops, plus up to 100,000 unlicensed (Innovision blog citing DGDA, undated) [uncertain]

  — [Innovision](https://innovision-bd.com/blog-news/from-progress-to-perfection-advancing-bangladeshs-pharmaceutical-sector-through-smarter-pharmacy-regulation)
- Dhaka, 246 pharmacies (2024): 93.48% were licensed, but only 7.56% had a registered pharmacist present. ~45% of antibiotics were sold without a prescription, 19.17% after informal dispenser consultation, and only 36.24% with a registered prescription (via search abstract) — [PMC12744235](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12744235/)
- About 50% of drug sellers lack formal pharmacy training — [PMC12142531](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12142531/)

**Labs**
- The ILAC MRA lists BAB as a signatory for ISO 15189 medical testing (signed 9 Jan 2020) — [ILAC](https://ilac.org/signatory-search/?q=b)
- Praava Health (2022) said it was "one of only five" labs in BD with ISO 15189 accreditation via BAB — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/266060/praava-health-receives-global-accreditation)
- icddr,b's ISO 15189 accreditation is from Thailand's BLQS, not BAB — [icddr,b](https://labservices.icddrb.org/accreditation)
- Medical technologists: 32,526 of 92,294 sanctioned MT-category posts are vacant. The category covers lab, pharmacy, radiology, physio and others (Bonik Barta, undated) — [Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/jlhin00R6bKyinX4)
- In 2020: 25,615 government physicians against 5,184 MTs and 1,417 lab technologists under DGHS. The WHO norm cited was 1 doctor : 3 nurses : 5 MTs (Financial Express, May 2020) — [FE](https://mail.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/editorial/shortage-of-nurses-and-medical-technologists-1588869305)
- No MTs were recruited at government hospitals in the 11 years to 2020 (Daily Star 2020) — [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/news/wholl-run-the-tests-1894342)
- A lab technologist shortage led to faulty sampling and inaccurate results (TBS) — [TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/77254)
- The PPRC study found diagnostic services disrupted by reagent shortages, broken machines and a lack of trained technologists. Only 22% of public facilities had essential medicines in stock — [TBS/PPRC](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/health/health-budgets-not-translating-services-due-governance-gaps-pprc-study-1501371)
- OpenMRS+ with the OpenELIS lab module runs in 100 public hospitals (97 on local servers) — [SHR DGHS](https://en.info.shr.dghs.gov.bd/hms/)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint is time per patient, not access to an app. Any tool must save time (e.g. auto-filling DHIS2/eMIS from clinical encounters) rather than add data-entry steps.
- There is no CME/relicensing mandate. Workers have no incentive to learn, which is why tele-mentoring/CME must ride on another need such as referral advice (the Vula model).
- The pharmacy gap is large: >150k outlets, very few with pharmacists present, and fewer than 300 model pharmacies. Drug-shop-level decision support plus data capture (the Maisha Meds model) is a large, under-served space.
- Violence data exist (CIPRB), but only ~25% of incidents are reported. A hospital incident-reporting and alert tool is a non-generic, policy-relevant gap.

### Gaps
- No 2019+ national OPD patients-per-doctor-per-day figure. The DGHS Health Bulletin probably has OPD totals but was not retrieved.
- No national CHCP time-use or workload study was found.
- A current BAB ISO 15189 medical-lab count was not found (bab.org.bd directory not fetched).
- Formal BMA central statements for 2025 were not retrieved.
- Current DGDA licensed-pharmacy count: sources conflict (107k–174k).
- The current status of the Bangladesh National Formulary (an edition after 2006, and any app) was not confirmed.

## Q2. Proven foreign digital tools for health workers, and whether Bangladesh already has each

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence-backed, non-generic imports are:
- ePOCT+ (protocolised CDS with point-of-care tests; antibiotic prescribing fell from 70% to 23%)
- Vula (structured specialist referral; 16-minute median reply)
- Telessaúde teleconsultoria (asynchronous second opinion; 56–77% of referrals avoided)
- Maisha Meds (drug-shop POS with clinical data)
- SMS lab-result return (Zambia)

Bangladesh has early or fragmented analogues for referral/telemedicine, pharmacy POS, CHW tools and lab LIS. It has nothing for protocolised point-of-care CDS for primary care or drug shops.

### Cited Findings

**1. ePOCT+ (Swiss TPH, Tanzania): digital IMCI-type CDS with CRP, Hb and pulse oximetry**
- Cluster RCT with 40 facilities and 44,306 children. Antibiotic prescription was 23.2% vs 70.1% in controls (adj. diff −46.4%). Day-7 clinical failure was non-inferior (aRR 0.97). No difference in deaths or hospitalisations (Nature Med, Dec 2023; NCT05144763) — [PMC10803249](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10803249); [Contagion Live 2024](https://www.contagionlive.com/view/prescribing-antibiotics-for-pediatric-outpatients-there-s-an-app-for-that)
- **BD status: NOT PRESENT** for UHC/community clinic OPD. Closest are the eRegMat CDS trial (maternal, Matlab) and a CKD protocol digital tool in development — [ISRCTN](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/ISRCTN69491836); [TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1385381)

**2. Vula Mobile (South Africa): structured referral and specialist-advice app**
- Cape Town (2019 data, publ. 2021): 14.5% of 13,321 ED patients were referred via Vula. 85.5% of referrals were accepted. Pre-transfer management advice was given in 35%. Median specialist reply was 16 min. Inappropriate ("green") referrals fell from 6.7% to 4.2% (p=0.004). About 14,200 users nationally (2017). Nurses were not using it — [PMC8517757](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8517757/)
- Used since 2014, now across >16 specialties. District doctors reported better coordination and professional development — [PMC9082220](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9082220/)
- **BD status: NOT PRESENT** as structured clinician-to-specialist referral. There are 234 DGHS telemedicine centres (patient-facing, >100k patients/year), but they were underfunded and staff went unpaid from mid-2024. A BSMMU specialised telemedicine line exists. No referral-acceptance or feedback loop was found — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1084557/bangladeshs-telehealth-centres-were-lifesavers-now-theyre-on-life-support); [Jago News](https://www.jagonews24.com/en/national/news/85000); [WHO SEARO 2023 BD slides](https://seaextranet.searo.who.int/meetings/TMwrshp/Shared%20Documents/Presentations/Plenary%20IV%20Telemedicine%20and%20digital%20health%20tools%20for%20COVID-19%20and%20beyond/SEAR%20TLM%20workshop-7-9%20Feb%202023-%20Plenary%20IV%20-%20Bangladesh.pdf)
- Informal WhatsApp/Facebook doctor groups probably fill this gap [uncertain; no source retrieved].

**3. Telessaúde Brasil Redes: asynchronous teleconsultoria for primary-care teams**
- First 24 months nationally (2013–15): 29,575 teleconsultations answered by 18 centres for 43,421 users in 9,801 units — [UFRGS LUME](https://lume.ufrgs.br/handle/10183/139752)
- Rio Grande do Norte/Paraíba (2014–17): 869 of 1,554 finished cases (~56%) avoided referral. Pará: 76.9% of referrals were avoided and >90% of users were satisfied (self-reported) — [RBMFC](https://rbmfc.org.br/rbmfc/article/download/3364/1815)
- Rio Grande do Sul (2012–13): nurses asked the most questions, then CHWs, then physicians. 95.26% were satisfied — [DOAJ RBMFC 2017](https://doaj.org/article/c7e7fe2e3f2b4593a97ba8df0a4b9194)
- Underuse is a known problem in Pará and Minas Gerais — same sources.
- **BD status: NOT PRESENT** (no asynchronous question-to-specialist service for CHCPs or UHC MOs was found).

**4. Medic / Community Health Toolkit (Kenya, Nepal and others)**
- Six MoHs (Mali, Kenya, Uganda, Nepal, Niger, Zanzibar) chose CHT for national community health (2021). In Nepal it supports >11,000 FCHVs, building on the 2012 Baglung pilot. A CHT app for community health nurses was added from 2022 — [Medic 2021 report](https://medic.org/2021-annual-report/)
- Supports >131,000 health workers globally (Q1 2024; Medic's own figures, which conflict with other counts) — [Medic Q1 2024](https://medic.org/q1-2024-impact-report/)
- **BD status: EARLY-STAGE.** Tools exist: the DGHS/DGFP eMIS for HAs/FWAs, the CBHC DHIS2 tracker (14,213 clinics) and an mPower CHCP system. They are reporting-centric, not workflow/CDS-centric — [Data4Impact eMIS](https://www.data4impactproject.org/publications/implementation-of-the-electronic-management-information-system-in-bangladesh-experience-and-lessons-learned/); [mPower](https://mpower-social.com/digitization-of-services-at-community-based-health-care-cbhc-under-the-government-of-bangladesh-and-integration-with-dghss-national-health-management-information-systems-hmis-human-resou/)

**5. D-tree Jamii ni Afya (Zanzibar)**
- A government-adopted digital CHV programme (2018). It reached national scale in Aug 2021 with ~2,200–2,300 CHVs serving ~1.9M people. It provides step-by-step protocol guidance, referrals and supervisor dashboards — [PMC10594132](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10594132/); [D-tree](https://www.d-tree.org/jamii-ni-afya/)
- **BD status: EARLY-STAGE** (same as CHT above).

**6. India CPHC-NCD application (Ayushman Arogya Mandir)**
- Apps for ASHAs, ANM/CHOs and PHC MOs. CBAC risk screening for ages 30+ covering HTN, DM and oral/breast/cervical cancer, linked to e-Sanjeevani teleconsultation and referral. As of 20 Jan 2023: 108,714 professionals trained, 26.23 crore enrolled and 14.40 crore screened — [MoHFW NCD portal](https://ncd-test.mohfw.gov.in/ncdlandingassets/aboutus.html)
- Challenges: connectivity, frontline capacity, and follow-up/drug availability — [PMC10112768](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10112768)
- **BD status: EARLY-STAGE.** The NCD desk guide is paper only. The community-clinic DHIS2 tracker covers cervical screening. No longitudinal HTN/DM tracking for CHCPs was confirmed.

**7. mSakhi (IntraHealth, Uttar Pradesh): ASHA job aid on CommCare**
- 153 messages. Quasi-experimental studies with 143 ASHAs (2012–13) reported better counselling and newborn classification. The government recommended scale-up. Evidence is mostly programme material, not peer-reviewed — [IntraHealth brief](https://www.intrahealth.org/sites/default/files/attachment-files/msakhibrieffinal.pdf)
- **BD status: EARLY-STAGE.** A newborn-assessment tablet app was tested with CHWs — [PMC6585142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6585142)

**8. WHO SMART Guidelines / Digital Adaptation Kits**
- The ANC DAK (2021) is software-neutral: personas, workflows, coded data elements, decision logic and indicators. Implementation studies exist in Ethiopia and Ghana — [WHO DAK ANC](https://who.int/publications/i/item/9789240020306)
- **BD status: NOT PRESENT / unconfirmed.** No Bangladesh DAK adaptation was found. DGHS has a BD-Core FHIR IG with a medication profile coded to the DGDA registry, which is a foundation a DAK could build on — [DGHS git FHIR IG](https://git.dghs.gov.bd/gitadmin/BD-Core-FHIR-IG/src/commit/03d49f4eb637f9d8b4cadc665dde2bf7493a8ad6/input/fsh/profile/BDMedication.fsh)

**9. Project ECHO (tele-mentoring)**
- Bangladesh appears only as spokes: Pallium India's Trivandrum hub (from Jan 2017; 18 spokes in India, Bhutan and Bangladesh), and palliative-care ECHO for clinicians in the Rohingya response — [Pallium India](https://palliumindia.org/?p=5815); [Cambridge PSC](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/palliative-and-supportive-care/article/using-project-echo-to-deliver-a-telementoring-and-teaching-program-on-palliative-care-in-south-asia-interpretive-description-of-participants-experiences-with-a-community-of-practice-for-learning/42B35FD4085181A8C8D1A8573F0B48E8)
- **BD status: EARLY-STAGE** (no Bangladesh-based hub found).

**10. Maisha Meds (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Nigeria, Zambia): free offline drug-shop POS that also captures clinical/sales data**
- Nearly 4,500–5,000 facilities (company figures). A USAID DIV grant of $5.25M (Nov 2023) targets 7,500 outlets. The company claims RCT backing, but no independent results were retrieved — [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/95587); [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/news/USAID-awards-Maisha-Meds); [Maisha Meds](https://maishameds.org/)

### Inferences: most promising non-generic gaps for the e-Health Card
1. A **"Vula-for-Bangladesh" structured referral and e-consult** service tied to the e-Health Card ID. It would let a UHC MO or CHCP send a referral to a district hospital, BSMMU or a medical college, with acceptance, advice and back-referral, filling the documented lack of a feedback loop. NOT PRESENT.
2. **ePOCT+-style protocol CDS** for UHC OPD and community clinics, built on WHO DAKs and BD-Core FHIR. It is designed for consultations under a minute and auto-generates DHIS2 reports, so it reduces data entry rather than adding it. NOT PRESENT.
3. **Drug-shop clinical POS** (Maisha Meds model) for model pharmacies and medicine shops. It would log antibiotic sales against e-Health Card IDs to give DGDA AMR surveillance and support the model-pharmacy compliance checklist. EARLY-STAGE only commercially.
4. **Lab-result push** from OpenELIS to the e-Health Card, the CHCP and SMS, with a standardised report template. NOT PRESENT.
5. **Health-worker safety incident reporting**: one-tap alerts and a national register (only ~25% of incidents are currently reported). NOT PRESENT [no BD tool found].
6. **CME tied to referrals**: Telessaúde-style asynchronous Q&A that logs CPD hours, aligned with future BMDC relicensing. NOT PRESENT.

- Rwanda/Malawi lab SMS and DrugStoc were not researched.

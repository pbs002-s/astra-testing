# 03 — Global gap analysis: proven foreign innovations vs Bangladesh status

**Categories**
- **(a)** patient/public
- **(b)** medical education
- **(c)** institutional operations. Tools for health workers are filed here as "(c) health-worker".

**Bangladesh status**
- **NOT PRESENT** = no Bangladesh equivalent was found in public web sources. This is not proof that none exists.
- **EARLY-STAGE** = a pilot, plan or partial system exists.
- **SATURATED** = a live government system, or several active players, already covers the core idea.

**Where the status comes from**
- Where the competitor check (note 06) and the earlier notes (02–05) disagree, the table uses **06** and explains the conflict in the Notes column.
- Impact figures are often company-reported. Those are flagged.

## What the table says

- **Closest to "proven abroad and absent at home":**
  - #12 QR registration
  - #30–32 closed-loop referral
  - #37 lab-result push
  - #42 equipment registry
  - #23 blood-stock visibility
  - #21 outbound feedback
  - #48 digital OSCE
  - #26–27 adult NCD decision support (the child IMCI version is taken)
- **Avoid:** telemedicine (#9–10), donor matching (#23), CHW data collection (#24), essential-drug stock dashboards (#36), and ambulance or volunteer dispatch as a stand-alone concept (#15–16).
- **Lessons from failures:**
  - Kilkari (#6): an RCT found no effect despite huge reach
  - M-TIBA (#14): the savings wallet shut down
  - Babyl (#9): the vendor collapsed
  - SISREG (#40): an audit found late and bypassed authorisations
  - e-Aushadhi (#36): an audit found implementation shortcomings
  - Body Interact (#50): no significant difference in its pilot RCT
  - **Common thread:** each reached scale or got built without showing it improved outcomes or kept working. Pitch outcome measures, connectivity fallbacks and government ownership from day one.
